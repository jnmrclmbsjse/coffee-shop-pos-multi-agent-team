# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Coffee-shop administrators and staff use this internal system during daily
service. Staff need a fast, touch-friendly point-of-sale workflow for taking
orders, managing the open business day, and recording operational activity.
Administrators maintain the catalog and review the records produced by those
workflows. The product currently serves one location and must remain clear as a
second branch is introduced.

## Product Purpose

UCM Coffee Studio POS keeps order capture, catalog management, manual inventory
counts, and business-day records accurate and auditable. Success means staff can
complete routine service tasks quickly, understand system state without
guessing, and trust that the screen matches the saved record.

## Brand Personality

Restrained, operational, trustworthy. The interface should feel calm under
service pressure, direct in its language, and familiar enough that the tool
disappears into the staff member's task.

## Anti-references

- Decorative consumer-commerce experiences that compete with operational data.
- Dense back-office systems with undersized controls or unclear state.
- A second visual language inside the staff POS shell.
- Novel controls, excessive animation, or color used without semantic meaning.

## Design Principles

1. Make the next operational action obvious and keep supporting detail nearby.
2. Show saved state truthfully, especially for money, availability, and order
   lifecycle changes.
3. Preserve one coherent staff workspace across order, inventory, and day
   workflows.
4. Prefer explicit labels and recoverable states over implicit or decorative
   cues.
5. Keep audit-critical facts visible without slowing routine service.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG AA contrast and semantic-state expectations. Support keyboard use and
visible focus even though the primary device is a landscape touch tablet. Every
operational control must provide at least a 44 by 44 CSS pixel touch target.
Important state changes and errors need clear visible text and appropriate live
announcements, and information must not depend on color alone.
